WHY THIS PROJECT MATTERS

Remembering Rizal is not enough.
We must think with him.

José Rizal was a writer, physician, and reform advocate whose works challenged abuses under Spanish colonial rule. His life reminds us that love of country involves more than admiration: it asks us to question injustice, learn carefully, and take responsibility for our choices.

This website reflects on both his achievements and the difficult questions surrounding his beliefs and decisions. We connect those lessons to modern Filipino life and to the ethical responsibilities of future information technology professionals.

01 / HIS IMPACT

Rizal's Contributions

Ideas can challenge a system, shape a shared identity, and inspire people long after their author is gone.

01

Literature that exposed injustice

Noli Me Tangere (1887) and El Filibusterismo (1891) portrayed abuses, inequality, and the consequences of colonial power. Through fiction, Rizal encouraged readers to examine the society around them.

Why it matters nowStories, journalism, and digital media can reveal injustice and help people question harmful systems.

Growth of Filipino national consciousness

Rizal's writing and participation in the Propaganda Movement helped articulate calls for rights, representation, and reforms. His work contributed to a growing sense that Filipinos shared concerns and deserved dignity.

Learning and service to others

As a physician, educator, and scholar, Rizal used his knowledge in service of people. During his exile in Dapitan, he engaged in community activities, including teaching and practical improvement projects.

02 / LOOKING CLOSELY

A critical look at Rizal

Rizal's preference for peaceful reform raises an important question: could gradual change succeed under a colonial system that repeatedly resisted reform?

What we recognize

Rizal generally pursued reform through education, writing, and civic action rather than leading an armed revolution. He feared that a poorly prepared uprising could cause widespread suffering. His position must be understood in the context of colonial repression, unequal power, and the political debates of the nineteenth century.

What deserves debate

His approach also had limits. Peaceful reform depended on authorities being willing to listen, while many Filipinos experienced abuses that made waiting for change seem impossible. The Philippine Revolution began in 1896, and Rizal was not its leader; he was arrested, tried, and executed by Spanish authorities that year. His death became a powerful symbol for the nationalist cause.

Our perspective

We should neither blame Rizal for not choosing the same path as other revolutionaries nor assume that his strategy was sufficient for everyone. His life shows that social change involves difficult choices, different forms of courage, and serious consequences. We can respect his contributions while still examining the limits of his ideas.

03 / THE PRESENT

Lessons for Today's Generation

Rizal's values become meaningful when we apply them to the challenges we face now.

01

Think critically before you believe or share

Rizal studied, observed, and wrote to challenge accepted narratives. Today, misinformation spreads quickly online. Check the source, compare reliable reports, and verify claims before sharing them.

02

Speak against injustice responsibly

His writings confronted abuses and unequal treatment. Young Filipinos can raise concerns about corruption and discrimination using evidence, respectful discussion, peaceful participation, and accountability.

04 / OUR REFLECTION

What does patriotism ask of us?

Studying Rizal challenges us to see citizenship as an everyday responsibility—not just a feeling of pride or a celebration on a national holiday.

His life reminds our group that loving the Philippines includes learning its history honestly, questioning unfair practices, respecting people whose experiences differ from ours, and contributing to the common good. We should not treat a national hero as someone beyond criticism. Instead, we can learn from his strengths while thinking carefully about the limits of his choices.

As future IT professionals, we will help build and maintain systems that influence how people communicate, learn, work, and make decisions. We can apply Rizal's lessons by protecting users' privacy, securing information, checking the accuracy of digital content, designing accessible technology, and refusing to use our skills to spread deception or harm others.

OUR COMMITMENT AS FUTURE IT PROFESSIONALS

  • Build technology with honesty and accountability.
  • Respect privacy, security, and human dignity.
  • Help people recognize misinformation and scams.
  • Make digital tools more inclusive and accessible.
  • Use our skills to support communities, not exploit them.
